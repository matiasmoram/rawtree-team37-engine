## Metric and evidence

Conversion is expected purchases per 100 requests, calculated by the simulator.
It is not the realized purchase rate. The automatic competition allocation is
50 points conversion, 10 cold-start conversion and 10 reliability; the published
100-point leaderboard rescales those 70 points. Conversion components are anchored
to baseline and oracle. `normalized_score_vs_reference` in the monitor is only a
diagnostic comparison against reference, not the official score.

The page explicitly supports `GET /api/state?by=model&from=EPOCH&to=EPOCH`:
15 minutes to 3 hours, in 5-minute steps. `scripts/analyze_windows.py` saves the
raw responses plus a CSV in ignored `logs/window_audit_*.{json,csv}`. Some windows
return only the ten most prominent versions; absence is not proof of no traffic.

## Reliability and attribution

The 15:50-16:05 common window had **100% on time**. The full hour had 85.40%,
including 53.70% in 15:05-15:20 and a later DNS outage visible in the log through
15:43. The best-model runner's first two minutes had no push failures or dropped
late responses; at 16:11 its reported p50/p95 were 0.96/1.42 seconds. These local
latencies are diagnostics; feedback and the leaderboard measure end-to-end service.

**Multiple independent emitters are a live issue.** A 5-minute query found 895
out of 897 submitted requests had two distinct versions. `t37-m1` submitted to
essentially every request; `t37-m2` subsequently appeared. Neither is implemented
in the local policy registry or run by this local process. No remote emitter was
stopped. Local traffic shares therefore do not guarantee the same credited shares
at team level. Coordinate a single writer or mutually exclusive request buckets
before making final experiment decisions. The two local Python PIDs of the runner
are the virtualenv launcher and its child, not two independent local runners.

The old feedback queries used `any(model_version)` by request. With two writers
this can credit the wrong model. The monitor, training loader and attribution
script now join both request ID and the **ordered shown list**. Identical lists
from different versions are `(ambiguous)`; unmatched team lists are `(unmatched)`;
fallback exposures have no credited model. Identical lists cannot reveal which
sender was accepted, so do not force an attribution. Old feedback CSVs and cached
training exports retain their old attribution; rebuild exports for model comparisons.
Leaderboard expected conversion comes from the platform, not this local join.

## Improvements, prioritized from code inspection and primary research

1. **Resolve the competing writers and preserve reliability.** A remote heartbeat
   alone is insufficient: use a lease/fencing or exclusive request ownership so
   two machines cannot both become active. A second host has not been provisioned.
2. **Use existing content neighbours for cold items.**
   `cache/new_product_neighbors.json` already exists, but serving never loads it.
   Add a separate candidate policy that borrows known neighbours' priors and
   co-visitation candidates for a new viewed item, and a reverse lookup to expose
   relevant new items. Keep similarity bounded, retain purchased-item penalties,
   and benchmark a cold-only slice. Generate missing embeddings outside serving.
   This is an adaptation inspired by [CB2CF](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.00384),
   not an implementation or expected gain established by that paper.
3. **Train a small ranker on corrected, recent v2/v3 exposures.** Expand candidates
   separately from reranking; add per-session similarity aggregates, source ranks,
   recency and price-relative features. [OTTO's third-place solution](https://github.com/TheoViel/kaggle_otto_rs)
   provides concrete candidate/reranker and weighted item-pair feature examples.
   Its GPU pipeline and offline recall objective do not transfer directly to this
   small real-time challenge. Our existing LightGBM AUC does not prove online lift.
4. **Calibrate recent top-slot feedback with shrinkage.** Begin with subcategory
   and position groups, then product-level estimates only when support is sufficient.
   Do not divide observed purchases by the heuristic EvidencePolicy score: it is
   not a calibrated probability. Use calibrated expected exposures and hold out
   time periods. [Top-N calibration research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11596)
   explains why calibration over all items can still fail on recommended items.
5. **Refresh co-visitation deliberately.** Contrary to the old live.py module
   description, `apply_delta` explicitly keeps co-visitation unchanged, and the
   cache refresher calls that same delta function. Its nominal daily full rebuild
   is not scheduled: a regularly refreshed cache stays young indefinitely. New
   products can enter the catalogue immediately but will lack fresh co-visitation.
   Test a separately decayed recent matrix. Since nocovis is strong, do not restore
   the old gain globally without a controlled comparison.
6. **Exploration after measurement is trustworthy.**
   [Deezer's carousel-bandit implementation](https://github.com/deezer/carousel_bandits)
   handles multiple slots, delayed feedback and browsing exposure. Use bounded
   exploration in slots 9-10 only in a separate experiment, not all live traffic.
   [Slate off-policy evaluation](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/5352696a9ca3397beb79f116f3a33991-Abstract.html)
   requires a correctly specified logging policy and support. A Thompson posterior
   draw is not itself the action propensity. Exact simple randomized probabilities
   are easier to audit initially. Automatic promotion needs sequential-testing
   controls; repeatedly checking an ordinary 95% interval is insufficient.

Device/region priors and subcategory/brand trends are plausible further variants.
The current evidence ranker uses session/history affinity and item-level trend,
but does not use device/region-specific candidate lists. Segment sparsity requires
fallbacks and smoothing. These ideas have not been shown to improve this team.
